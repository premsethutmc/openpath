# Supervised pilot protocol: five capabilities (draft)

**Status: a draft for the people who will run the pilot. It is not approved.**

Before any child takes part, three groups must agree to it in writing:
- the responsible educator;
- the organisation hosting the sessions (school, tutoring centre or family group), including its safeguarding lead;
- a data-protection reviewer for the country of the pilot.

Where this draft says *decide*, the value is a proposal.

The software does not need accounts or a server for this protocol. It runs in a browser on a device the supervisor
controls, and keeps every answer on that device. Nothing is sent anywhere. Live evaluation of writing (E3b) and
remote storage stay switched off.

## 1. Purpose and limits

| What the pilot can show | What it cannot show |
|---|---|
| Whether children can use the app under supervision | Whether the app teaches effectively. This pilot has no comparison group and is too small |
| Whether the five-capability journey runs as designed: a first check, a lesson with practice, a fresh confirming check, and a later check | Whether its judgements about a child are accurate. Every content item is a candidate awaiting educator review |
| Where content, wording or the app fails | Anything about a child's ability that should be reported to a school or used to place a child |

The five capabilities:
- spelling inflected forms;
- subject–verb agreement;
- articles;
- finding details in a text;
- the main idea.

No result from the pilot is a grade or a measure of a child. It must never be presented to anyone as one.

## 2. Who can take part

| Item | Proposal |
|---|---|
| Age | *Decide* (decision D1 in EDUCATOR_DECISION_PACK.md). Proposed: 10–12. The capability framework is written for ages 10–15, and the pilot content is mostly at the easier levels (difficulty 2–4). Children younger than 10 are not in scope until an educator confirms the content suits them |
| Reading | The child can read short sentences on their own. The app reads nothing aloud except in spelling dictation, where speech is available |
| Number | Proposed: 5–10 children. Enough to find usability and content problems, not to measure anything |
| Not included | A child who does not want to take part, or whose guardian has not given written consent. Also any child for whom the supervisor or school has a welfare concern that makes a new activity unsuitable at that time |
| Special needs | A child with a known reading or learning difficulty takes part only if the guardian and the educator agree and the supervisor knows. The app has no adaptations for this, so the supervisor watches more closely |

## 3. Parent or guardian consent

Written, before the first session, on an information sheet in plain language. The sheet says:
- what the app is, and that it is a test of the software and the content, not of the child;
- that the questions and lessons have not been approved by an educator yet;
- what is recorded:
  - the name or nickname typed into the app (proposal: a code such as "P07", never the full name);
  - every answer;
  - when it was given;
  - the app's estimates;
- that answers stay on the device. Nothing is sent over the internet, and no account is created;
- the supervisor's notes (section 8) and how long they are kept;
- that taking part is voluntary, and that the guardian can withdraw the child at any time without giving a reason
  (section 7);
- who to contact, and the safeguarding contact.

The consent form records:
- the child's code;
- the guardian's name and signature;
- the date;
- separate yes/no answers for:
  - taking part;
  - the supervisor keeping written observations;
  - exporting the child's app record for the review in section 9 (optional; the pilot works without it).

## 4. Child assent

At the start of the first session, and briefly at every later one, the supervisor explains in the child's language:
- "This is a new reading and spelling app. We are testing the app, not you."
- "Some questions may be too hard or not quite right. That is the app's fault, not yours."
- "You can stop whenever you want, and nobody will mind."

The child says or shows that they agree. A child who does not want to take part, or who later wants to stop, stops.
The supervisor records the assent (yes, or a later stop) on the session sheet. A guardian's consent never overrides a
child's "no".

## 5. Sessions

| Item | Proposal (*decide*) |
|---|---|
| Length | At most 20 minutes of app use, then stop even if the session is unfinished. The app saves after every answer; "Save and leave" ends a session at any point |
| Frequency | At most one session a day and three a week. A later check is due about 7 days after something is shown |
| Content | Only the five capabilities. The supervisor starts the activities the Today screen offers for them and does not open other parts of the app |
| Device | One device per child, set up by the supervisor: a recent browser, the pilot build installed, sound on for spelling dictation. No other app open |
| Setting | A quiet place the child knows, with the supervisor in the room and able to see the screen |

**Stopping rules.** The supervisor ends the session with "Save and leave" when any of these happens:
- the child asks to stop, or shows they want to (looks away for a long time, pushes the device away, says "I can't");
- signs of frustration or distress (section 6);
- 20 minutes have passed;
- the app shows something wrong: an error, a frozen screen, a judgement the supervisor thinks is plainly wrong (log it,
  section 8), or anything unsuitable;
- the child has answered five questions in a row wrong. Also tell them: "These were hard ones. Let's stop here."
  Five wrong in a row usually means the content is too hard or unclear, not that the child should keep trying.

## 6. Supervision, frustration and distress

**The supervisor:**
- is an adult approved under the host organisation's safeguarding procedures;
- stays in the room throughout and can see the screen;
- does not answer for the child, give hints or say whether an answer is right. The app's own hints are recorded as
  help, which is correct, but help from a person is not recorded at all;
- if the child asks for help, says: "Do what you think. It's fine not to know." If the child is stuck, ends the
  question with "Save and leave" rather than helping;
- reads the parent overview's adult recommendations (for example, "ask a teacher to look at this") but does not act on
  them during the pilot. They are recorded for the educator.

**Frustration or distress** includes tears, anger, repeated "I'm stupid" or "I'm bad at this", refusing to go on, or
anything that worries the supervisor. Then:
1. Stop the session.
2. Reassure the child: the app is being tested, the questions may be wrong, and they did well to try.
3. Record what happened, without the child's name, on the session sheet.
4. Tell the guardian the same day.
5. Do not resume that day. Ask the child, and the guardian, before any further session.

Any safeguarding concern, whether or not it is about the app, follows the host organisation's safeguarding procedure.
It goes to its safeguarding lead, not into the pilot records.

## 7. Data: minimisation, retention, withdrawal and deletion

**What exists:**

| Record | Where | Contents | Kept until |
|---|---|---|---|
| The app's record | In the browser on the pilot device only | The code (e.g. "P07"), every answer with its time, lessons seen, hints | Deleted at the end of the pilot, or on withdrawal (*decide* the end date; proposed: within 2 weeks of the last session) |
| Exported record (optional, with consent) | An encrypted folder held by the pilot lead | The same as above, in a backup file | Deleted after the review in section 9 (proposed: within 3 months) |
| Consent forms | Held by the host organisation | Guardian's name, signature, child's code | The organisation's own retention period for consent records |
| Session sheets and failure log | Held by the pilot lead | The child's code, observations, no names | Proposed: deleted within 3 months, or kept only in anonymised summary form |
| The code-to-name list | Held by the host organisation only, never with the app records | Codes and names | Deleted at the end of the pilot |

**Minimisation:**
- Use a code, never a name, in the app.
- Do not photograph or record the child. Screenshots of a problem must not show the child or other names.
- Do not export the app record unless the guardian agreed to it and the review needs it.
- Writing practice is not part of the pilot. Nothing a child writes is sent anywhere: live writing evaluation is off.

**Withdrawal and deletion.** A guardian or child may withdraw at any time.
1. In the app: open the learner, go to Settings, and choose "Delete this learner from this device". This removes the
   record from the browser.
2. Delete any exported copy.
3. Delete the session sheets and failure-log entries for that code, or keep them only if the guardian agrees.
4. Confirm the deletion to the guardian in writing.

**Data-protection review.** Rules on children's data differ by country. For example, India's Digital Personal Data
Protection Act, 2023 treats everyone under 18 as a child and requires verifiable parental consent, and the UK has the
UK GDPR and the Age Appropriate Design Code. A reviewer for the country of the pilot must check this protocol, the
information sheet and the consent form before any session (decision D11).

## 8. Recording software failures

The supervisor keeps a failure log. One line per event:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Date and time | 2026-11-12 10:41 |
| Child's code | P07 |
| Device and browser | Android 14, Chrome 131 |
| What the child was doing | "A fresh check on articles, question 3" |
| What happened | "The keyboard covered the answer box"; "the same passage appeared twice"; "the app said 'found hard' after 3 of 4 right" |
| Severity | blocks (the session could not continue), misleads (a judgement or message seemed wrong), minor (cosmetic) |
| What the supervisor did | Saved and left; carried on; restarted the browser |
| Screenshot | File name, if one was taken (never showing the child) |

**"Misleads" entries** are reviewed under section 9. **"Blocks" entries** stop further sessions on that device
until the developer has looked at them.

## 9. Manual review of questionable learning judgements

The app's labels and plans are drafts. Examples are "shown", "found hard", "improving", "not kept", and "teach main
idea next". Until an educator has reviewed the rules, every judgement is provisional. During the pilot:

1. **Nothing the app says about a child goes to the child, the guardian or the school as a result.** The supervisor
   may tell the guardian how the sessions went, in their own words.
2. **The supervisor flags** any judgement that seems wrong from what they saw. For example:
   - the child plainly knew it but the app says "found hard";
   - the child guessed and the app says "shown";
   - the plan sends the child somewhere unhelpful.

   The flag goes in the failure log as "misleads".
3. **The educator reviews each flag** against the record. The parent overview's details list the evidence behind
   every recommendation: the answers, whether each was on the child's own, with help, straight after a lesson, or on
   a passage already counted. The educator decides one of:
   - the judgement was right;
   - the content was at fault (record it on the review CSV as reject or revise);
   - the rule was at fault (record it for decision D6–D9).
4. **Patterns go back to the developer** as issues. A rule changes only by a reviewed, versioned change. It is never
   changed during the pilot.

## 10. Before the first session (checklist)

- [ ] The educator has reviewed every row the pilot will show a child (the review CSV). At minimum:
  - each pilot capability's first-check, practice, confirmation and later-check questions at difficulty ≤ 4;
  - their passages;
  - the lessons.
- [ ] The educator has decided D1–D10 in EDUCATOR_DECISION_PACK.md, or accepted the proposals in writing.
- [ ] The host organisation and its safeguarding lead have agreed to this protocol.
- [ ] The data-protection review (D11) is done. The information sheet and consent form are approved.
- [ ] The pilot build is installed on each device and checked with a test learner, who is then deleted.
- [ ] Session sheets and the failure log are ready, and the code list is held apart from them.
