## What was built

### 1. Framework 0.2.0 (`content/english/framework/en-0.2.0.json`)

- **39 core capabilities specified:** a description, band descriptors at 2, 4, 6 and 8, misconception
  lists, observable formats and references. Generated by `tools/english/authoring/framework-0.2.0.ts`
  from 0.1.0, so the 17 skeleton capabilities are word for word unchanged.
- **References, indicative only.** OpenPath's descriptors remain the definitions.
  - Each capability names the CEFR Companion Volume (2020) scale(s) it relates to, such as
    *Grammatical accuracy* or *Orthographic control*.
  - Where a capability covers a topic in the CBSE Class X syllabus (2025–26), it names it: Determiners,
    Tenses, Modals, Subject–Verb Concord, Reported Speech, the formal letter, and the analytical
    paragraph.
  - NCERT's *Learning Outcomes at the Elementary Stage* (2017) is cited at document level only. I
    could not verify capability-level outcomes, so I do not claim them.
- **No new prerequisites.** The references I checked list topics by class, not dependencies between
  them, so adding prerequisites would mean inventing them. The 6 E1 prerequisites are kept and marked
  as having no cited source.
- **19 candidate couplings** between the new capabilities and related ones (strength ≤ 0.35), marked
  *candidate*. Like E1's, they are expert guesses.
- **Scale, prior and policy are unchanged.**

**Why 0.2.0 is not yet the runtime default.** If it were, the 39 newly specified capabilities would
have no items until E2b:

- goal-level uncertainty would stay above the assessment threshold, so the scheduler would never leave
  assessment;
- every strand summary would be pulled towards the 4.0 starting value;
- the frozen simulation would stop being comparable.

So 0.2.0 is used by the content checker, the coverage report and the carry-over test, and becomes the
runtime default in E2b, when its bank exists. The rule is stated in `tools/english/load.ts`.

### 2. Formats

| Format | Scoring | Guessing floor |
|---|---|---|
| `order:` | the whole order must be right | 1/n! |
| `match:` | every entry matched correctly | 1/(n!/(n−k)!) |
| `multi:` | exactly the right set chosen | 1/C(n, k) |
| `typed:first_letter`, `typed:correction`, `typed:short_answer` | the existing typed scorer, against accepted answers | 0 |

- **No partial credit**, so the update mathematics are untouched. The floor enters as the existing 3PL
  *c*, and a test proves an update through the new formats equals the existing update.
- **Order items:** the format is implemented and tested, but the skeleton capabilities did not need
  it. Sequencing capabilities in E2b will use it.

### 3. The bank

- **Volume:**
  - 124 new items and 4 new passages, at bands 2, 4, 6 and 8: *Tara and Bobby*, *The Volcano*, *How
    Honeybees Choose a Home*, and *The Map in My Grandfather's Drawer*.
  - 247 items in all, 34 format variants, 103 low-guessing items (2–13 per capability).
- **Difficulty ladders** (`content/english/items/progressions.json`).
  - Each capability has an explicit ladder: the features that make its items harder, in order, each
    at a band and under one of its band descriptors.
  - Example (subject–verb agreement): third-person -s, then plural subjects, compound subjects,
    intervening phrases, indefinite pronouns, *either … or*, and finally inverted order and *the
    number of*.
  - An educator can review 17 short ladders rather than hundreds of rationales.
- **Every item records:**
  - its ladder step, and a rationale for its difficulty;
  - `difficulty_source: expert_estimate`, and `review: candidate-unvalidated`.

  The 123 E1 items gained the same metadata. A check confirms no scoring field changed, so existing
  learner histories still score identically.
- **Families:** 27 families with variants (55 items) give retention checks somewhere to go other than
  an exact repeat.

### 4. Tools

- **`node tools/english/bank/main.ts`** checks all content under both framework versions and writes
  `tools/english/out/bank-coverage.html` and `.json`. It now runs in CI.
- **It fails** on malformed content, or when a capability misses a gate that is not recorded with a
  reason in `content/english/items/coverage-gaps.json`. A recorded gap that has closed also fails, so
  the gap list cannot go stale.
- **The coverage report shows:**
  - each capability's difficulties on the 1–8 scale, its gates and its ladder;
  - every item with its rationale, easiest first;
  - the retention runway, and passage readability signals.

## Difficulty progression: what is validated, and what is not

**What the checks enforce, mechanically:**

- Every item sits within 0.75 band of its ladder step.
- Ladders are strictly ordered, and each step is within 1 band of its band descriptor.
- Every descriptor has at least one step, and every step has at least one item (G7).
- Variants in a family share a step and a format, and lie within 0.5 band of each other.
- Difficulties cover each capability from band 2 or below to 7.5 or above, with no gap wider than
  0.75 band (G3–G5).

**What this shows:** the difficulty labels are internally consistent and follow an explicit,
reviewable ordering of what makes items harder. **It does not show the labels are right.** Only real
learners' responses can do that.

**One signal that needs a human.** The rough readability signals for passages do not rise steadily
with band. *The Map* (band 8) has fewer long words than *Why Do Cities Get Hotter?* (band 5); its
difficulty is in implied meaning and abstraction, which word length does not measure. Passage bands
need an educator's judgement.

## The coverage gap: retention runway (G8)

**Test:** for a learner at each level from 2 to 7.5, after 6 items of typical use on a capability,
can the bank serve 4 retention checks on unseen items or unseen variants? On average it serves
**0.97 of 4**.

**This is not only a 14-item limit.** I measured it on synthetic banks of evenly spaced typed items:

| Items per capability | Variants per family | Engine as built | If assessment and learning used one item per family |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 48 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| 24 | 3 | 0 | **4** |
| 48 | 3 | 3 | **4** |

*Each cell is the minimum number of checks served across all levels.*

**Why:** assessment and learning use the same items near the learner's level that a retention check
needs. Adding items alone hardly helps. Reserving variants for retention, by having assessment and
learning use one item per family, would let about 24 items per capability, in families of three,
pass.

That is a **selection change (E4)**, so I have not made it. Until then the engine falls back to
flagged repeats at least 14 days apart, which never change the estimate (E1 final review). I
recorded the gap rather than writing weak or redundant items to chase it, as you asked.

**Improvements, and why:**

- **M2 and M3:** easier and harder items give weak and strong learners informative evidence (L2's
  foundations: 2.42 against a true 2.0, where E1 gave 3.04).
- **M5:** more items per grammar capability leave room for adaptive choice.
- **The ceiling:** L7 (true 7.5) is now estimated at 6.76, where E1 gave 6.34.
- **Exhaustion:** about 11 of 17 capabilities ran out of items per learner; now about 0.

**Regressions, and why:**

- **M6, the guesser (L9).**
  - L9 answers at random 30% of the time, and reading is underestimated by 1.21.
  - The engine's response model has a guessing floor but no allowance for careless or random slips.
    On the new low-guessing items (typed, match, multi), a random answer is almost always wrong, and
    because those items are more informative, each one pulls the estimate down further. E1's mostly
    four-option items blunted this.
  - The criterion was already fragile in E1: it passed on 3 of 10 seeds.
  - This is a **model limitation that a better bank exposes**, not a content error. Fixing it means
    adding a slip parameter, or a person-fit check that flags erratic answering. Either changes the
    update mathematics, which you asked me to preserve, so it is a decision for you (below).
- **M1.**
  - On the frozen seed it falls to 0.40, mostly because of one near-flat profile: L8's true strands
    differ by at most 1 band, and its rank correlation swings from 0.85 to −0.78.
  - Over 10 seeds the fall is small (0.61 to 0.57), and the allocation of the first 40 items across
    strands is essentially unchanged.
  - As the E1 final review found, 40 items over 17 capabilities is not enough for this measure under
    either bank.
- **M4** still passes on the frozen seed. Over 10 seeds the mean is slightly lower, and one seed is
  just under 70%.

## Existing tests changed (set-ups, not criteria)

The bank changed, so four E1 tests' set-ups no longer held. In each case I checked the engine before
touching the test.

1. **Exposure test helper:** it could only answer choice and typed items; the SVA bank now has a
   multi item. The helper now answers every format.
2. **E1-5 (revoke equals never applied):**
   - **What changed:** the test no longer compares the exposure flag. Estimates, evidence counts and
     stages must still match exactly.
   - **Why:** a revoked answer withdraws the scoring decision, but the learner still saw the item, so
     a later sibling is correctly flagged as a variant. In the history without that answer, the
     sibling is fresh.
   - **When it appeared:** item families, new in E2a, made this visible.
3. **Easier-step test:** it assumed no SVA item suits a learner at 6.5 once three were used. E2a added
   suitable ones, so the set-up now uses up every SVA item from 5.5 upwards.
4. **Demonstration test:**
   - **The old check:** Asha and Ben differ by more than 0.5 on the *sentence strand*. That depended
     on how a few answers fell. Asha got one run-on item in 40 under both banks.
   - **The replacement:** the capability-level standard you approved for E1-13 in R3. They separate
     on articles, in the direction of their profiles (5.49 against 3.75; Ben is the weak-grammar
     learner).
   - **What still fails:** Asha's own weakness (run-ons) does not separate after 40 items.

## Issues found in E1 content (logged, not changed)

- **A second grammatical answer.** Some E1 typed items accept only a present form although a past
  form would also be grammatical (for example `grm.sva.01`, "My brother ___ cricket"). Accepting more
  answers changes scoring, so it needs a new item version. The ledger rejects evidence for unknown
  versions, so old versions must stay loadable first. Logged for E2b, not done here.
- **"My uncle, who lives in Delhi, is visiting us"** (`grm.com.03`) assumes one uncle. With more than
  one, the clause could be defining. Same constraint.
- **New items:** I fixed eight such problems in my own new items during review, before committing:
  - a non-defining clause about "my grandfather" (a child has two), now about Mount Everest;
  - three proofreading items with a second valid one-word fix, which is now accepted;
  - four verb prompts where a past form would also have been grammatical, which now ask for the
    present form or give a clearer cue.
