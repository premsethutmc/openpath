## What was built

- **Eight new practice topics in Grammar in use** (34 sentences): prefixes and suffixes, verbs that take an object, sentences in order (whole sentences as tiles),
  similes, idioms, words for cooking, sport and yoga, an opinion and its reason, words for feelings and actions.
  Each opens from its syllabus line.
- **Tasks with a grown-up** (60 tasks, `content/english/guided/tasks.json`): for the lines an app cannot mark,
  which are speaking, finding things out, and reading a book of one's own. The learner does the task aloud or
  away from the screen; an adult ticks what they saw; the ticks are kept on the learner's record
  (`guided_task` events). The screen says plainly that the app does not hear or mark these.
- **Writing lines** (four in Class 4, four in Class 5, nine in Class 6) now open the essay studio, a separate part of the app. They stay "partly": its tasks
  are not yet matched to this class.

## What "partly" still means here

- Dictation of a whole unseen passage: sentences are dictated one at a time, not a passage.
- The four writing lines, as above.

## Limits

- A tick from an adult is a record, not a measurement. It changes no level.
- The suggested literature is published work and is not in the app; the learner reads a book of their own and
  tells an adult about it.
- Class 6 has 19 lines that are only partly covered: nine are writing; the rest include metaphor and personification, sorting verbs into transitive and intransitive, and reading between the lines.
- All eight classes are loaded: 253 lines, none without a place (109 practised in the app, 20 partly, 124 with a grown-up).
- Class 1 points to First words (see FIRST_WORDS.md) for reading with pictures, everyday words and three- and four-letter spelling; it added 28 tasks with a grown-up (166 in all) and alphabetical order by first letter.
- Class 8 is mostly writing (13 of its 37 lines wait on the essay studio) and analysis of literature, so only two lines are fully practised in the app.
- Class 2: the lines are placed, but the present first levels of the app are hard for a child of that age. A beginner activity (pictures and sound, single words before sentences) is still to be built.
- Classes 2 and 8 added two practice topics (kinds of noun, the main clause) and 48 tasks with a grown-up (138 in all).
- Class 7 was read from a web page that sets out the syllabus, not from the pages of the document, so it has no page numbers and may be less complete than the others.
- Classes 3 and 7 added five practice topics (the subject of a sentence, collective and material nouns, alphabetical order, synonyms, antonyms and analogies, sound words) and 30 tasks with a grown-up (90 in all).

## Fifteen half-covered lines filled (19 November 2026)

A fourth kind of sentence was added to Grammar in use: a question about a sentence with two or three answers to
choose from. With it, and a few more sentences of the older kinds, these became fully practised:

- transitive or intransitive; kinds of adjective; the predicate; phrase or clause; kinds of subordinate clause
- figures of speech by name (metaphor, personification, alliteration, pun)
- formal and informal wording; working out a word's meaning from its sentence
- the simple future; joining two sentences into one

Still partly covered (70 lines): about 40 writing lines; whole-passage dictation; reading lines that need new
texts (poems, prediction, theme, mood); two-step directions and action words in First words; tense kept steady
across a paragraph; pronouns with each and everyone, where usage is disputed and no rule was forced.

## Eight more lines filled (19 November 2026, second round)

- **Dictation of a passage** (Classes 3, 4 and 5): six short passages of four sentences, dictated one sentence at
  a time in order. For "a passage already seen" the passage is shown to read first and hidden when dictation
  starts. A passage sitting does not move the learner's level.
- **First words** (Class 1): sixteen action and describing words (run, swim, hot, cold), and an eleventh step,
  a two-step direction ("tap the cat, then the apple"). Giving directions aloud stays unheard, so that line is
  still partly covered.
- **Reading skills on short extracts** (Classes 6 to 8), as choose-the-answer sentences: what comes next, mood
  and tone, how a text is built, what speech shows about a person. These are practised on two or three
  sentences, not on whole texts, and the syllabus lines say so.

Still partly covered (62 lines): about 40 writing lines; following a theme through a long text; poems, speeches
and dialogues to listen to; tense kept steady across a paragraph; pronouns with each and everyone.

## Writing, class by class (19 November 2026)

All 42 writing lines now open writing tasks set for their class: 84 tasks, two per line, in
`content/english/guided/tasks.json`. Each has a length to aim for (from 30 to 80 words in Class 1 up to 250 to
300 in Class 8), a self-check list for the learner and a tick list for a grown-up. Kinds: paragraphs and
compositions, stories, personal experience, informal and formal letters, applications, invitations, notices,
posters and advertisements, arguments, descriptive and creative pieces, poems, and the plan, draft, revise
process.

- The learner can type in the app, with a running word count, or write on paper.
- **The app does not keep or mark the writing.** Only the word count and the ticks are recorded. A "copy my
  writing" link lets the learner keep the text elsewhere.
- The essay studio and its evaluator were not changed. They remain the place for a closer analysis of a piece.

Still partly covered (20 lines): reading that needs whole long texts; poems, speeches and dialogues to listen
to; a few lines where one half is spoken; tense kept steady across a paragraph; pronouns with each and everyone.
