# Level by competency, not by class (November 2026)

Status: candidate content, practice-only. Levels are OpenPath's estimates; nothing is educator-reviewed.

## Why

A learner's class or age does not say what they can do in English. A Class 12 student or an adult may read well
and still not be able to say which word is the noun. Each skill is placed by answers, on its own scale.

## What changed

- **Grammar is part of Find my level** (part 5 of 5, six questions, tapping only, nothing typed):
  1. find a noun, 2. find a verb: asked of everyone, whatever their level;
  3–6. four sentences on a staircase of the six grammar levels, starting in the middle, a wide first move
  (two levels), then single steps. Each is either a kind of word to find or one wrong word to tap.
  The level is the highest one answered right. The two basics are recorded separately.
- **Naming words is a new kind of practice** in Grammar in use: "Tap a noun in this sentence." 24 sentences on
  levels 1 to 4 (noun and verb; adjective and pronoun; adverb and preposition; conjunction and harder cases).
  A level sitting holds at most two of them, beside three to fix and three to build.
- **When the basics were missed**, the Grammar row on the home screen says "Start with naming words: noun and
  verb" and opens a sitting of that topic, until one such sitting is finished. The level found is kept: a learner
  can be at level 5 for sentences and still be sent to the naming words first.
- **Every part of Find my level starts in the middle of its scale**, not from the learner's age. Age is used only
  for a part the learner skipped (the result screen says so).
- Grammar in use starts at the level the check found.

## Limits

- Six questions give a first estimate only. Tapping can be guessed (about one chance in six per sentence).
- The longer engine grammar check (15 to 20 questions) is still separate and still offered.
- Texts are still written for children. See ADVANCED_LEVELS.md for the top levels and for what is missing below level 1.
- In a naming-words sentence every word of the kind asked counts as right; "the" and "a" are not accepted as
  adjectives.
