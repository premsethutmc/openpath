# Gandhi and the mass phase: from Dandi to Quit India

Status: curriculum map and production plan. The playable Quit India lesson now covers the wartime context through the separate 1947 ending at `frontend/history/quit-india/`. The approved Dandi build is unchanged. Draft PR #288 only; no merge or deploy.

## Chapter scope

The ICSE Class X Mass Phase of the National Movement (1915–1947) is the reference spine. The entire section is not a Gandhi biography and Quit India is not another name for the Salt March.

| Lesson | What learners should understand | Format / status |
|---|---|---|
| Gandhi's method | Satyagraha and nonviolence; use Champaran as an introductory example, not a claim that this example is required by every board. Explain how a local grievance can become public collective action. | Short visual opener; planned. |
| Non-Cooperation | Rowlatt Act and Jallianwala Bagh, Khilafat background, withdrawal of cooperation, impact, and suspension after Chauri Chaura. | Interactive choices showing what it means to withhold cooperation; do not reenact violence. Planned. |
| Civil Disobedience | Reaction to the Simon Commission and Purna Swaraj at Lahore, 1929. Why salt, how it was used to break a specific law, and how participation and repression spread. Gandhi–Irwin Pact, Second Round Table Conference, and renewal. | Dandi visual journey and optional political context exist. Add a short bridge later for campaign programme, impacts and renewal; the journey is not the full chapter. |
| Quit India | World War II context, threat from Japan, failed Cripps Mission, demand for immediate British withdrawal, arrests, how the protest continued locally, suppression, and significance. | Complete core story in draft; mobile and learner review gates are separate. |
| Beyond Gandhi | Forward Bloc and INA, then Cabinet Mission, Mountbatten Plan, Independence and Partition. Distinguish those parallel and later elements from what Quit India directly achieved. | Separate short lessons; not part of the first production slice. |

CISCE Class VIII: teach causes, sequence and impact; keep advanced constitutional/political detail optional. ICSE Class X: causes, resolution and significance of Quit India are core. Do not require memorising clauses of the Cripps proposal. Tamil Nadu Class X, Volume 2, Unit 8 covers the national Gandhian phase; Unit 9 §9.5(b) and §9.6 add Vedaranyam and the local Quit India experience. CBSE Class X map overlay includes Dandi; do not imply that Quit India is a listed CBSE map requirement.

## Playable episode: The Morning After

Teaching aim: *Why didn’t the movement end when its leaders were arrested?*

Use a fictional young observer, not a historical character or a saboteur. Target 4–5 minutes for the core at ordinary reading speed; optional source cards extend it. Match Dandi: minimal text, documentary-style imagery, period sound, no scratch narration, no quiz gate, no personal data. Do not commission an actor or use an unlicensed recording. Use dates to show time jumps; do not imply that every event was on the same morning.

1. **Why now? · 1942.** Open on two short pieces of evidence on a table: a map showing the context of Japan’s advance in Asia and an editorial card contrasting the failed wartime negotiations with Congress’s demand for immediate withdrawal. Each tap reveals a plain sentence. Use authored paraphrases, not counterfeit reproduced newspapers. Optional TN note: shortages and rising prices added to public discontent.
2. **The call · Bombay, 8 August.** Look towards the gathering at Gowalia Tank Maidan. On-screen, the AICC adopts the Quit India resolution and Gandhi calls for “Do or Die.” Use the authenticated phrase in a caption, no invented Gandhi dialogue, no voice imitation. One line clarifies his call for nonviolent struggle and immediate independence. No recording presented as genuine without provenance.
3. **The empty stage · 9 August.** Switch to a quiet street in the morning. Tap the notice: Gandhi and other senior leaders arrested; Gandhi detained at Aga Khan Palace. No uniform or arrest role-play. The lesson’s question now has a concrete cause.
4. **Turn the radio dial · Later in 1942.** A radio listening scene in the fictional observer’s home. Tuning reveals an optional captioned, sourced note about Usha Mehta and colleagues using Congress Radio despite censorship. Use radio static only; no reconstructed voices needed. The dial is a playback metaphor, not instructions to build transmitters or evade officials. Explain that local action, not just the radio, kept the movement going.
5. **What people did · Select places.** An optional map reveals local demonstrations and strikes, underground communication, and examples of parallel administrations in Ballia, Tamluk and Satara at different times. Add a Tamil Nadu card about students and workers participating and the textbook’s account of K. Kamaraj continuing to organise. A Madurai note can acknowledge the documented clash without reenacting it or giving it a score. Represent repression and incidents of violence factually; do not equate Gandhi’s call with every action taken.
6. **What changed, and what did not.** The authorities suppressed the uprising; it was a major demonstration of the demand for independence, but Britain did not leave in 1942. Show a distinct jump to 1947, note that independence had multiple causes and came with Partition, and link forward to a separate lesson. Optional ungraded reflection: “What two things kept the movement going after the arrests?” A learner can compare an answer with a short model without submitting it.

## Implementation order and finish line

1. Build the bridge as a small, separately linked timeline using the existing Dandi route and source language; do not modify the accepted opening or the existing offline file.
2. Prototype the August 8 → August 9 → later-1942 radio passage. It must be understandable with sound muted and no preselected quiz.
3. Add the four-place map, including Tamil Nadu, the aftermath, and the optional comparison of Non-Cooperation, Civil Disobedience, and Quit India. Keep the child’s agency in where to look and what evidence to inspect, not changing history or acting out harm.
4. Confirm each board claim against its named edition. Source and distinguish quotation, paraphrase, archive, reconstruction. Test phone widths at 320/390 px, keyboard alternatives, reduced motion, replay and source panel. Verify a grade-eight learner can retell cause → resolution → arrests → local response → significance; do not claim learner evidence until tried with learners.
