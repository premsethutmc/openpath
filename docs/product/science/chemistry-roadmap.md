# School chemistry: curriculum and build order

**Scope:** Primary science foundations through Class XII chemistry, including practical reasoning. This is a curriculum design and sequencing map, not a claim of complete coverage or validated learning. Build and test one finished learner experience at a time on the Science Labs draft branch. No production merge or deploy. No learner accounts, paid model calls or external student data for the samples.

## What changes as the learner progresses

| Band | What is taught | What the learner should be able to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | Familiar materials; comparing properties; water, air, and visible changes. These are part of broader environmental science, not a separate formal chemistry course. | Choose a material for a purpose and say what observation supports the choice. |
| VI | Materials and properties, solubility, and choosing separation methods such as sieving, settling, filtration and evaporation. | Separate components of a mixture, explain why each tool works, and explain what passed through a filter even when the liquid looks clear. |
| VII | Acidic, basic and neutral substances, indicators and neutralisation; physical versus chemical change. | Use evidence to classify an unfamiliar sample or change, not just recall a colour or reaction name. |
| VIII | Particle model of matter; elements, compounds and mixtures; solutes, solvents and solutions; introductory comparison of reactivity. | Distinguish dissolving from disappearance, and mixtures from compounds. Use a particle picture to explain a visible result. |
| IX | Atomic structure and models; isotopes; atoms, molecules, ions, valency and formulas; conservation and combining ratios; mixtures, solutions, colloids and suspensions, and more separation techniques. | Translate between an observation, a particle diagram, a formula and a measurement. Choose a method based on the relevant property. |
| X | Reaction evidence, equations, conservation, reaction types and introductory redox; acids, bases, pH and salts; metals, extraction and corrosion; carbon bonding, organic families, soaps and detergents. | Predict a result, balance its representation without changing substance identities, and explain the prediction using ions, reactivity or bonding. |
| XI | Measurement, mole and stoichiometry; atomic and electronic structure; periodic trends; bonding, geometry and intermolecular forces; thermodynamics; dynamic and ionic equilibrium; redox; organic structures, nomenclature, isomerism, mechanisms and hydrocarbons. | Calculate amounts with units, reason about structure and behaviour, and distinguish energetic favourability, equilibrium and rate. |
| XII | Physical: solutions, electrolyte behaviour, cells, electrolysis and kinetics. Inorganic: transition elements and coordination. Organic: halogen compounds, oxygen and nitrogen functional groups and their transformations; biomolecules. | Analyse data and curves, predict products within taught rules, explain cell/ion behaviour, and justify an unknown identification from evidence. |

**Practical track:** Read an instrument, select a comparison, repeat and record observations, then explain measurement limitations. Introductory separation and indicators progress to preparation/purification, chromatography, calorimetry, rate comparisons, volumetric analysis, salt identification and viva/reporting where the board prescribes them. Virtual activities prepare a learner for, but do not certify or replace, supervised practical work.

## Build families in this order

| Order | Family of experiences | Transfer evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Mixtures, separations and particles.** Salt/sand bench first. Later reuse tool selection for magnetic separation, chromatography, crystallisation, and choosing evaporation versus distillation depending on which component we want. | Pick a method on a new mixture and explain why clear filtrate may still contain dissolved material. |
| 2 | **Acids, bases and salts.** Indicators with an unknown, then particles and pH, then virtual titration and concentration when the learner has the prerequisite mathematics. | Identify an unknown from evidence, distinguish concentration from acid strength, and later interpret endpoint data. |
| 3 | **Atoms, bonding and formulas.** Build an ion or molecule, compare shape and polarity, explain why materials behave differently; use tables and diagrams for periodic trends, not a lab for every trend. | Interpret an unfamiliar formula or structure and justify a prediction. |
| 4 | **Reactions, mass and quantities.** Observe evidence, account for atoms including gas in a closed system, balance, then move to moles, limiting reactants and yields. Use step feedback for coefficients versus subscripts and unit errors. | Independently solve a new reaction case and explain what limits product. |
| 5 | **Physical and analytical systems.** Change one variable and inspect linked records: energy, rates, equilibrium, solutions, electrochemical cells, titration and qualitative evidence. Teach data interpretation and experimental controls, not just apparatus clicking. | Explain a new graph or diagnose an unknown using several observations. |
| 6 | **Carbon and materials.** Structure builder plus transformations for homologous families, naming, isomerism, properties, mechanisms and biomolecules. Use expandable process diagrams for metallurgy and 3D diagrams only where geometry teaches something. | Infer a property or a likely taught transformation from structure. |

A lesson can link **what I see → what particles are doing → what I write or measure**; expose only the views that help at that age. Use a compact scene when the student can manipulate a meaningful variable, a diagram when the relationship is the point, and worked examples and fresh practice when the skill is symbolic. Keep visuals simple until the full journey works; improve the ones worth revisiting. No general chemistry engine is needed to support the first lab. Extract shared components after real repetition.

## First sample: Where did the salt go?

- Anchor: NCERT Class VI separation. The optional particle explanation is an extension, not a requirement to know ion notation before using the lab.
- Goal: separate two solids using their different solubility, then recover each in the simulation. Make the result visible: sand stays behind; a clear salt solution comes through; water leaves and crystals remain.
- Teaching response: give meaningful results for a premature filter or evaporation choice, then let the learner recover. A particle view explains why a filter does not take out dissolved salt. The scene is idealised, not a real lab procedure or a drinking-water claim.
- Done for prototype: all controls work on touch and keyboard, recover from wrong order, a new question tests the underlying reason, no clipped scene at 320/390 px, and the other three labs keep working. Prototype success is not independent learner mastery.
