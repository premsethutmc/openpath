The Lantern on the Hill

Mia lived in a narrow house at the bottom of a steep hill. Every evening she watched a tiny light flicker at the very top, where nobody had climbed for years.

"It is only a reflection," said Tom, her older brother, without looking up from his book. Mia was not convinced. She noticed that the light appeared at exactly the same moment each night, just after the church bell finished ringing.

On Saturday morning Mia packed a rucksack with sandwiches, a torch and a notebook. Tom sighed, but he followed her through the gate anyway. Their neighbour, Mrs. Patel, waved from her garden and warned them that the path was muddy after the storm.

The climb was harder than Mia had imagined. Brambles scratched her knees and the ground squelched beneath her boots. Halfway up, Tom stopped beside an enormous oak tree and pointed at something carved into its bark. It was an arrow, faded but unmistakable, pointing towards the summit.

At the top they discovered a crumbling stone hut. Inside, hanging from a rusty hook, was an old brass lantern. Its glass was cracked and its handle was wrapped in string. Mia opened her notebook and carefully copied the strange letters scratched on its base.

Tom picked up the lantern and turned it over. A small brass key fell out and rattled across the floor. Mia caught it before it disappeared through a gap in the boards.

When they returned home, Mrs. Patel was waiting at the gate with a mug of cocoa. She studied the key for a long time. Then she explained that her grandfather had been the lamplighter in the village, and that he had promised to keep a light burning on the hill for sailors lost at sea.

Mia realised that someone was still keeping his promise. That evening, when the church bell finished ringing, the light on the hill flickered once again, and Mia smiled.
