Timer and clues

Recommended default. A thin ring around the clue button (the light bulb) fills slowly towards the next offer. There are no numbers, no countdown and no warning sounds. The ring measures active thinking time only. It pauses while the word is being said, while feedback is on screen, when the app is hidden or the phone is locked, and when a grown-up pauses it. It does not reset when a clue is taken; the next offer simply waits at least 7 seconds. Offers also wait until the child has stopped typing for 3 seconds, so a child who is working is never interrupted.

The bulb can be tapped at any time. The timer decides only when OpenPath gently offers help (the bulb glows and a small bubble asks “Want the first letter?”). Nothing is ever given automatically, and the whole word is never revealed by a clue.

A word is copied with guidance only when, after two tries, the word is shown and the child copies it. There is no percentage threshold. Thinking time is recorded, but only to time offers of help; a slower answer never counts against the child. These timings are starting hypotheses, not validated thresholds. A grown-up can choose “Give more time” (all offers 60% later) or “Only when asked” (no offers, no ring), and can hide the ring.

How the input stops whole-word suggestions

The child types with the phone's own keyboard into a real text box that sits invisibly over the letter tiles. OpenPath keeps its own copy of the answer and, after every change, compares the box with that copy:

  • One new letter at the end is accepted (this is how ordinary typing and Android composition arrive).
  • Letters removed from the end are accepted (Backspace, or Backspace held down).
  • Anything else is rejected and the box returns to the child's own letters: a paste or drop, a tapped keyboard suggestion, autocorrect replacing the word, several letters at once, or a letter put in the middle. The cursor is kept at the end.

Comparing values works the same for every keyboard. Key events are not used, because Android keyboards report most keys as “Unidentified”. The browser's own hints are also set: autocomplete, autocorrect, capitalisation and spell check are all off.

A rejection is never counted against the child. The tiles give a small shake. After two rejections a quiet line says “Type one letter at a time”. Each rejection is recorded only for diagnosis, as rejectedInputs.

Not yet verified on a real phone. Samsung Keyboard and Gboard may still show a suggestion strip; they ignore some of the browser's hints. The prototype's keyboard buttons send the same events a phone does, and the unit tests cover them. Real behaviour still has to be checked on the Samsung phone before anything is built.

The evidence record for one word

One compact record per word, shown live in the prototype under “What OpenPath records”. It is a proposed new event, spell_attempt_v2. Today's Spelling Bee answers are not evidence for the learning engine, and this design keeps it that way: no mastery calculation changes in this phase.

Decisions for you

  1. The timer as a faint ring on the clue buttonNo numbers; it pauses automatically and continues after a clue. The alternatives are above.
  2. Offer timings of 8, 15 and 25 seconds as the starting pointPlus “Give more time” and “Only when asked” for grown-ups.
  3. Sentence and meaning still count as on their ownNeither gives letters. Any letter given makes the word “with help”, and exactly which letters were given is recorded.
  4. Two tries, then “write it together”The word is shown and copied once. The alternative is a third try with more letters.
  5. Where meanings come fromEach word needs a child-friendly meaning. Either write and review them, or start from the word bank's dictionary glosses (337 of 818 words have one; adult wording).
  6. Targeted help after the same slip on 2 different wordsIt is treated as a possible difficulty, never a confirmed misconception. With less evidence, help goes left to right.
  7. Words that sound alike say their sentence from the startFor example their, there and they're.
  8. The grown-up view lives under ProgressIt is not on the child's screen.
  9. A real-phone check of letter-by-letter input before any buildOn Samsung Keyboard and Gboard.
